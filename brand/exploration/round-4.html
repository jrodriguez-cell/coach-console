Round 4 · lower letters · one spacing rule

What changed from your feedback

  • Lower letters: the letters are less tall relative to their width. Section 01 shows four steps. My pick is 2 : 1: every letter is exactly twice as wide as it is tall.
  • Same height, same width, equal spacing: every letter is the same box, and every gap between letters is exactly one letter width. The two lines are one letter height apart.
  • The monogram uses the same spacing: M T / T M has exactly those gaps, one letter width across and one letter height between rows.
  • Thin, like Michroma: the stroke stays at Michroma’s 12% of the letter height. A finer 9% version is in section 03.
Still open: (1) A centred or B nested. With this rule, B becomes a perfect checkerboard: every letter of TOMOVE sits exactly under a gap. (2) Letter proportion. (3) Weight. Turn on Show letter boxes to see the equal boxes and gaps.
SPACING RULE  ·  letter = W × H  ·  gap between letters = W  ·  gap between lines = H  ·  monogram uses the same gaps
At 2 : 1  →  W = 2 units, H = 1 unit: letters 2 wide, 2 apart; lines 1 apart.

04Monogram · same spacing

M T / T M with the wordmark’s exact gaps: one letter width between columns and one letter height between rows. Like the wordmark, it is wide rather than square, so it sits as a band across the circle. That is the honest cost of matching the spacing exactly. Thin strokes go soft at 32px, so the small cut (heavier strokes, same letters and spacing) takes over at 64px and below. At 16px, use the small cut and treat it as a texture.
